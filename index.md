## Acelynn Qiao

[aqiao@ucsd.edu](mailto:aqiao@ucsd.edu)

**Section:** D19 – Mapping Body Gestures to Sound

**Mentor:** Victor Hugo Minces

**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**  
The most interesting topic for me was how much you can change a sound while keeping its pitch the same. In my vowel experiments, I kept the same fundamental frequency and used filters to emphasize different frequency ranges. I could hear the sound shift from one vowel to another even though the pitch stayed steady. As a dancer, that made me curious about what would happen if different parts of a movement could control different parts of a sound. A step could cue when a sound starts, for example, while the way I move my arms could shape how it sounds.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**  
I’d like to explore whether whole-body gestures can be used to communicate a small set of messages through sound. For example, a camera could recognize a movement involving a step, a shift in the torso, and an arm motion to trigger a short phrase like “come here.” At the same time, I could use my phone as a handheld controller to shape how the pitch rises or falls, so the same phrase could sound calm or urgent. I’d start with a few phrases and two intended tones, then play the audio for listeners without showing them the gestures. I’d track how often they recognize the phrase and how often they recognize the intended tone.

**What is a potential change you’d make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**  
One of the projects I’ve been building this quarter is called Motion Score. It’s a web app that uses my phone’s motion sensors to control a synthesized sound in real time. Tilting the phone forward or backward changes the pitch, and tilting it side to side changes how many harmonics contribute to the sound, making it sound simpler or richer. I’d extend that setup by adding camera input so I could use my whole body to choose a sound or phrase and keep the phone as a separate control for how it’s delivered. I’d also want to see whether the phone can tell the difference between an intentional tilt and the motion that naturally happens when I take a step or turn.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**  
I’d like to try camera-based pose tracking to follow joints like the shoulders, elbows, hips, and knees. I’d compare recognizing a gesture from a single pose with recognizing it from a short sequence of poses. Two gestures might end in the same position, but a sequence could capture whether someone stepped forward or backward to get there. I’d also like to test whether smoothing the phone’s sensor readings makes pitch control steadier during movement and whether that introduces a noticeable delay.
